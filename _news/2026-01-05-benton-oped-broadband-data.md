I published an op-ed at the Benton Institute for Broadband & Society titled [What We Can't See, We Can't Fix: The Journey to Build Independent and Accessible Broadband Data](https://lnkd.in/eQZnX87i). The piece lays out our vision for building an independent broadband data infrastructure that enables researchers, policymakers, and advocates to verify broadband access claims with ground-truth evidence. It traces the evolution of our work from the original Broadband-Plan Querying Tool (BQT) to the more capable BQT+, which uses AI agents to collect address-level data on service availability, quality, and pricing from ISP web interfaces. This effort has been deeply collaborative, and I want to acknowledge the contributions of Udit Paul, Laasya Koduru, Elizabeth Belding, and Tejas N. Narechania, whose work has been instrumental in advancing this research.
